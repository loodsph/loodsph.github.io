A *stack trace* is what you read when something breaks: it tells you the sequence of calls that led to the error. It forces you to trace back the causal, logical chain, step by step, until you find where things went wrong.

Here I would like to apply the same method to ideas. For me, thought must be a reflection of reality.

I spend a considerable part of my days in a lab in contact with chemical substances. It’s an activity that requires a certain cognitive effort. If you want molecules to do what you want, you have to understand the rules of the game. You have to think, understand, and try. Make mistakes, reason, and try again. It is reality that must shape thought, not the other way around. Detaching from reality, simply, doesn't work.

Outside, however, it seems we have stopped reckoning with it. This "attachment to reality" – understood as a complex system requiring patience to be decoded – has faded away.

Smartphones and social media? Without a doubt. It doesn't help to have the biggest companies in the world "against" you, whose business model revolves around keeping us passively glued to a screen. But it's not just a problem of analytical capacity, it's a matter of penalizing diversity. And this is not a good thing: in Darwinian terms, diversity is the most effective adaptation (and therefore survival) strategy, besides being the only intellectually stimulating thing. In practice, we are measuring the value of an idea based on how much it reflects the tastes of the masses: what could we expect if not a [world of mediocrity](https://www.alexmurrell.co.uk/articles/the-age-of-average)?

Social media has only amplified a [pre-existing problem](https://loodsph.github.io/complexity/filosofia/psychology/2026/01/13/bugs-bunny.html).

This blog is my attempt to think in public, [slowly](https://ofdollarsanddata.com/theres-always-room-for-quality/), about things that interest me, knowing full well that thoughts are always a work in progress. I don't write to put a period, but to have a direction. What deeply interests me is the method. This blog is not an editorial project, it has no plan, it has no target audience. It doesn't look for readers: it filters them.

If a post didn't teach me something while I was writing it, I don't publish it.

The basic policy is: Zero cookies, zero tracking, no algorithms. I don't track anything, not even the number of visits. If I am talking to the cosmic void, so be it.
